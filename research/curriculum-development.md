- Curriculum emerged as a term in 1576, can be simplified to mean "course of study"
- includes what will be explicitly taught through the topics of study, and the implicit lessons from the environment such as the structure of the curriculum

- Smith, M. K. (1999). ‘Aristotle on knowledge’, The encyclopedia of pedagogy and informal education. <https://infed.org/dir/aristotle-on-knowledge/>.
    - Aristotle's three-fold calssification of disciplines
    - disciplines can be theoretical, productive or practical
    - telos/tele, or purpose, of any particular form of knowledge determines its appropriateness
    - slightly disagree that the telos of theoretical sciences is knowledge for its own sake as even exploratory fields like pure mathematics can eventually lead to practical outcomes - like a shotgun spread that may hit something even if most of the pellets go wide
    - theoretical thinking (read: contemplation) is the goal - all other thinking must be done in service of increasing the body of knowledge one has access to to enable greater(?) contemplation
    - productive thinking: eidos (the plan/idea), techne (the process of craft), poietike (the making action?), the product
    - practical thinking is about producing a process (rel: praxis)
        - the point of the thinking is to improve the thinking itself?
